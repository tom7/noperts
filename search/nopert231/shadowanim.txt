shadowanim: almost passing through itself (the canyon)
=====================================================

What it shows
-------------
A polyhedron is Rupert if a copy of it can pass through a hole in it: seen
from some direction, the shadow of a slightly turned copy fits strictly
inside the shadow of the original. The animation shows the two shadows for
Candidate #231:

- the original's shadow, filled (blue-grey);
- the turned copy's shadow, outlined (white): the copy is rotated by a tiny
  angle (1e-7 radians) about the axis that helps it most, then slid sideways
  as well as possible (an exact linear program);
- in red, wherever the copy's shadow sticks out of the original's.

At this angle the two shadows coincide to the eye, so the left half shows
the whole shadow and the right half three magnified insets, centered on the
three places where the copy sticks out most (circled on the left). The
magnification is the same in every frame, so the size of the red is
comparable across the whole animation.

Phase 1 (12 s). The viewing direction travels from an ordinary view to the
"canyon": the direction from which #231 comes closest to passing through
itself. The approach slows down exponentially, because the canyon is very
narrow. The red shrinks from a clear band to nothing visible: at the
canyon, the copy sticks out by only about 3e-4 of the rotation angle (3e-11
here, a tenth of a pixel even in the insets). Seen from there, a quadrilateral
face is almost edge-on, and two of its corners are nearly hidden behind the
silhouette.

Phase 2 (8 s). The view stays at the canyon, and the axis of the copy's
rotation swings away from the best one and back. The red reappears: only
rotations very close to that one axis come close to fitting.

What it means for the proof
---------------------------
The red never disappears completely: to first order, every small rotation
of the copy sticks out somewhere, by at least (angle x margin), where the
margin depends on the view and is smallest (about 2e-4 to 4e-4, depending
on the side) at the canyon. The proof's "identity tube" turns this into a
certificate: near the identity rotation, three contact points (the circled
places) block every small rotation, with an explicit radius. The canyon is
where those certificates are thinnest and the computer search had to work
hardest.

How it was made
---------------
nopert229/shadowanim.cc (branch nopert231). The canyon direction is
(0.9151, 0.2020, 0.3490) in the frame of ruperts/nopert_231.stl; viewmap's
--margin output lists it with the object rotation that shows it in Blender.
Usage: ./shadowanim.exe --out canyon.mov [--width 3840 --height 2160]
  [--seconds1 12 --seconds2 8] [--theta 1e-7] [--canyon x,y,z] [--start x,y,z]
Frames are lossless PNG in a .mov at 60 Hz (codec/mov.h).
