tieviz: the deltoidal hexecontahedron's special poses
=====================================================

Every picture has the same layout. On the left, the whole "shadow" of the
deltoidal hexecontahedron (DH) seen along a view direction u: the projection
of the solid onto the plane perpendicular to u, outlined in bright green
(faint green fill and faint green projected polyhedron edges). On top of it,
in red (bright outline, faint fill and edges), the shadow of a rotated copy
of the same solid (the "inner" copy). Rupert's property asks for a pose in
which the red outline fits strictly inside the green one; a part sticking out
would be filled solid red. On the right, insets magnify the places where the
red outline comes closest to the green one (three, or two when the picture
is mirror symmetric and a third would repeat one). Small white circles mark
vertices of the red outline lying exactly on the green one (touching). The
picture is centrally symmetric, so each inset also stands for the opposite
side.

Stills (3840x2160 PNG)
----------------------

tie_b015.png, tie_b005.png, tie_b020.png, tie_b015_a002.png
  "Pinning ties." Take the plane through the center that contains two
  opposite 5-fold vertices and two opposite 2-fold vertices, and reflect the
  DH in that plane. The reflected solid is not the DH, but it is a close
  relative: 4 of its vertices are the DH's own (the ones in the plane), 8
  more land exactly on other vertices of the DH, and 16 land exactly on the
  DH's edges. For views u in a wedge next to the plane's normal x, the
  shadow of the reflected solid lies inside the DH's shadow and touches it.
  The reflection composed with the reflection perpendicular to u (which does
  not change the shadow) is a rotation, so this is a real rotated copy of the
  DH: the red shadow fits, but only with ties — it touches the outline at
  about ten places and nowhere has room to spare. The file names give the
  view's position in the wedge (b = distance from x along the wedge, a =
  sideways); the rotation angle is twice the angle between u and x (5.7
  degrees at b = 0.05, 17 degrees at b = 0.15, 23 degrees at b = 0.20).
  These are exactly the poses where the computer search stalls: it can prove
  that nearby poses do not work only if it reasons exactly about the touching
  points.

mirror_u0.png
  The simplest tie. Near a 2-fold axis (the view here is 0.1 rad off one),
  the plane perpendicular to the axis is a mirror of the DH, so the
  reflected solid IS the DH, and the rotated copy's shadow equals the
  original shadow exactly: every vertex of the outline touches (white circles). This
  one is harmless (a symmetry argument removes it), but it is why the 2-fold
  views are hard.

fold_eta0.001_passage.png
  The punchline. Push the twelve 5-fold vertices of the DH outward by 0.1%
  (keeping all its symmetry; the kite faces fold very slightly). The ties
  open up: in the pose shown (near the b = 0.15 tie, rotation 17 degrees)
  the red shadow fits strictly inside, with gaps of about 7e-5 (the insets
  are magnified 50 times more than in the tie pictures). So this slightly
  folded DH is Rupert: a copy of it passes through a hole in itself.
  dh_boundary.py checks this pose in 60-digit arithmetic. Pushing the
  vertices inward instead makes the ties worse. The DH sits exactly on the
  boundary.

Animations (3840x2160, 60 Hz, lossless PNG .mov)
------------------------------------------------

wedge_sweep.mov (12 s)
  The view slides along the wedge from almost exactly x (rotation angle
  0.2 degrees) out to b = 0.2 (23 degrees), always with the tie rotation.
  The shadow changes shape and the rotation grows, but the red copy
  always fits with ties and never with room to spare. Near the start the
  rotation is tiny and the two shadows nearly coincide: that end of the
  wedge is the "cap" where the computer proof needs second-order (curvature)
  arguments.

fold.mov (8 s)
  The view and rotation stay near the b = 0.15 tie while the 5-fold
  vertices are pushed outward from 0 to 0.1%; at each frame the pose is
  re-optimized (best view and axis at a fixed 17-degree rotation). At the
  start the copy touches (the exact DH); as the solid folds, every contact
  opens into a gap, growing in proportion to the fold. Insets at the same
  magnification as fold_eta0.001_passage.png.

Blender
-------

tie_b015_{outer,inner}.obj, tie_b015_view.txt and fold_eta0.001_*: the DH
(outer, unrotated) and the rotated copy (inner, same center) as OBJ meshes
in the model frame; the view.txt gives the view direction (put an
orthographic camera on +u looking at the origin) and the image axes. With a
light shining along u, the inner copy's shadow on a screen behind lies
inside the outer one's, touching (tie) or with tiny gaps (fold).

Usage: see the comment at the top of tieviz.cc.
